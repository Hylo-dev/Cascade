# Addon process resource observations

The internal runtime includes metric reading and reduction, shared CPU accounting, health, and control of new admissions. These paths can be verified with controlled adapters and host-supplied bindings; the production connection to authenticated processes and the native stop remain blocked. They introduce no autonomous timers.

The caller supplies the PID, the process birth instant, the executable UUID and the local identity of the measurement. The reader compares birth and UUID in the same record that contains the counters. A measurement that is missing, rejected or attributed to a different identity is not turned into zero usage. These checks do not authenticate the publisher, do not necessarily distinguish every new execution of the same image, and grant no authority to terminate a process.

The first valid sample provides only the current footprint. From the second compatible sample on, the increments of the user and system counters are computed separately, then their sum is converted from Mach ticks to nanoseconds using the actual timebase. Intermediate products use double-width arithmetic; unrepresentable results are rejected. Aggregate CPU greater than the elapsed time is possible on multiple cores and is not artificially capped at 100%.

The [choice on RAM attribution](../../.scratch/cascade-product/issues/64-addon-memory-attribution.md) assigns the footprint only to the verified identity of the measured
physical process. A shared service does not copy it to direct or transitive consumers, even if
their canonical interest is active; the process and the physical total stay counted only
once. The subsequent [provider policy](../../.scratch/cascade-product/issues/65-addon-provider-memory-policy.md) defines thresholds, episodes and actions for that process alone. The UI budget shared with the provider remains the baseline; calibration and semantics of the UI profile require separate evidence; these choices do not enable native identity, reading or stop.

Every acquisition keeps a before/after time interval around the native call. The CPU interval is an estimate between ordered acquisitions, not an instantaneous snapshot. Missing samples, invalid times and overflow break continuity. A counter regression establishes a new base; a changed identity or an observed process that has already exited invalidates the measurement until a new binding is explicitly created. The reset planned for the reader's future owner does not re-enable an invalidated identity.

The internal coordinator groups explicitly supplied bindings and keeps one reducer for each. The observation capacity is limited to 1,024 rows and does not constitute process admission. A single monotonic deadline arms the first sample after registration, limits the periodic cycle to 1 Hz, and is recomputed from the current instant without recovering lost ticks. Explicit samples at job boundaries or on memory pressure do not move this deadline. The coordinator exposes the next deadline but installs no timer or task; synchronous reads are serialized in its actor and produce bounded batches that keep the exact binding. A mismatching or already exited identity is reported as unavailable and withdraws only that registration.

No exit data or ESRCH error automatically frees runtime reservations. Still to be integrated: the canonical, authenticated native binding between process and addon, the qualified OS source that drives the shared cycle, the production connection to health decisions, and the actually observed stop. The internal runtime already composes the deadline and the CPU reaction described below. The unit conversion is backed by Apple sources, synthetic tests and an [independent calibration with getrusage](../superpowers/verification/2026-09-18-addon-process-cpu-calibration.md) on the macOS 27 arm64 diagnostic process. All samples fell within the reference's acquisition intervals; this does not qualify the absolute precision of kernel accounting or the clock. The declared compatibility of the public APIs is not the same as evidence on macOS 14 or on every architecture.

Unit sources: Apple [rusage filling](https://github.com/apple-oss-distributions/xnu/blob/xnu-10002.1.13/osfmk/kern/bsd_kern.c#L1144), [task Mach counters](https://github.com/apple-oss-distributions/xnu/blob/xnu-10002.1.13/osfmk/kern/task.c#L6055), [libproc public API](https://github.com/apple-oss-distributions/xnu/blob/xnu-10002.1.13/libsyscall/wrappers/libproc/libproc.h#L99).

## Shared CPU credit for event-driven addons

The [burst decision](../../.scratch/cascade-product/issues/46-addon-cpu-burst-policy.md) sets 100 ms of initial/maximum credit and a refill of 5 ms per monotonic second. `AddonCPUBudget` charges the observed CPU time, keeps the debt, and rejects invalid instants or an unrepresentable charge without changing the account. The clock stays `Duration` even below the nanosecond; it does not use civil time. The refill is a calculation, not proof that a measurement is available.

The coordinator accepts an explicit, host-verified `eventDrivenOwner`; a generic registration remains observation-only. The accounts are separate from the bindings and indexed by verified publisher and addon ID, with a default capacity of 32 and a maximum limit of 1,024. Several processes of the same addon consume the same account. Binding removal, observational exit, a new provider and the reset on wake keep the credit/debt. There is no automatic eviction of accounts and no per-job reset; retention applies to the coordinator's incarnation, without persistence across a Cascade restart.

Intervals are charged inside the same actor that produces them, with no entry point for external batches to replay. The output contains a single CPU observation per owner at the end of the round: `complete(snapshot)` if every one of its bindings supplied a charged interval, `incomplete` with no balance if even a single measurement is missing, or a persistent `accountingFailed` if the arithmetic cannot represent a charge. Valid intervals are still charged when another binding has a missing measurement. An accounting error keeps the last accepted account and prevents a later sample from looking successful after a charge was skipped.

`complete` describes only that round and the registered bindings: it does not authenticate the owner, does not recover lost intervals and does not declare the profile healthy. The internal reaction to overruns and the shared deadline are described below; the production connection and native enforcement remain separate. There is no automatic application of the event-driven credit to continuous scenes or audio. [Verification of the continuation](../superpowers/verification/2026-09-20-addon-cpu-credit.md).

## Classifying a new CPU overrun

The [21 September decision](../../.scratch/cascade-product/issues/49-addon-cpu-violation-counting.md) counts new usage beyond the credit, at most once per owner and shared round. The coordinator adds the classification to the same accounting observation: `moderate` when a charged positive interval leaves a negative balance; `noNewViolation` when the complete measurements do not prove a new overrun; `unavailable` when there is not enough evidence or the account is in error. Several processes are aggregated without summing potentially overflowing `UInt64` counters and without keeping a duplicate history.

A negative balance with a CPU interval of zero does not produce another incident. A positive measurement that suffices to prove the overrun, on the other hand, still does so when another binding is incomplete: the balance stays hidden in the incomplete accounting result, while the evidence of known usage is not erased. Periodic rounds and explicit ones at job boundaries or on pressure apply the same criterion. This classification does not by itself apply reductions, stops or quarantine. [Verification](../superpowers/verification/2026-09-21-addon-cpu-violations.md).

## Internal connection to health

`AddonRuntime` owns a coordinator and a health store for its own lifetime. An internal hook registers the observational binding for a canonical incarnation of the provider: verified identity and version derive from the host catalog, while the health session is issued by the store at the startup handoff and reused by the metrics registration. Registration can precede the protocol connection, so as to include the startup cost. There is no entry point for external batches or for health credentials supplied by the addon.

Registration and sampling share a limit of a single operation in progress. The runtime captures instant, incarnation, binding and session before the wait, then verifies the same authority again before recording an incident. Stop, disable, close and exact exit revoke the logical authority; observational detachment does not free physical reservations. Debt and history are kept across provider restarts. Stale samples and rejected health writes have distinct outcomes.

A valid new overrun goes through the existing store, which returns `keep` for the first two incidents and records the version's quarantine at the third within the closed 300-second window. The path stays internal and does not prove a native stop; retries after a classified crash are described separately below. The subsequent control of new admissions is described below; the [verification of the health connection](../superpowers/verification/2026-09-21-addon-cpu-violations.md) keeps the evidence and limits of that tranche.


## Temporary rejection of new jobs

The [first approved alternative](../../.scratch/cascade-product/issues/52-addon-cpu-reduced-admission.md) is applied in the runtime with a bounded set of verified identities. A current overrun closes new admissions even if writing the incident fails; only a later sample that is complete, current and has strictly positive credit can reopen them. Credit of exactly zero, missing/stale samples and errors do not reopen them. The version's quarantine is not cleared by credit recovery. The mere passage of time and a provider restart do not reset the block.

New actions and new provider jobs receive `resourceDenied`, with no new queue, retained reservations or automatic replay. Duplicates recover the previous state. Actions already accepted by the journal/scheduler and invocations already admitted can run and complete with their original deadlines. The checks concern the effective provider and are repeated after the waits, before commits or deliveries; the shared paths for output, completion, release and cleanup remain available.

An acquisition can reuse a canonical source that has already been started: the broker atomically distinguishes this case from a source that is new, to be restarted, or still to be started. If the provider is already blocked, a new start is rejected before the commit; if the overrun arrives during a v1.4 commit, the existing indeterminate-outcome semantics remain and the subsequent start is not delivered. The host bootstrap can recreate the process after an exit to allow new measurements, keeping the debt and the ban on new work. These hooks do not enable the launcher.

The cost attributable to the consumers of shared services follows the [approved conservative decision](../../.scratch/cascade-product/issues/55-addon-delegated-cpu-attribution.md), described below; the per-process measurement is not a precise per-request measurement. [Verification of the continuation](../superpowers/verification/2026-09-21-addon-cpu-admission.md).

## Shared deadline and runtime wake

The internal metrics integration uses a fifth stable identifier in the runtime's aggregate queue; the other four remain publications, actions/cold start, asset transfers and services. `nextDelay` composes the coordinator's deadline, rechecks authority after the waits, and transfers the five identifiers when the first eligible addon changes. Metrics with no rows disarm themselves. No autonomous timer or macOS observer is installed.

`serviceDeadlines` first completes the job deadlines and the shared cleanup, then serves at most one pending reset and one periodic round. The coordinator keeps the limit of one round per second and does not generate catch-up for skipped ticks. Explicit samples at a job boundary or on memory pressure do not move the periodic cadence. A busy measurement, or a pending reset whose deadline is already due, postpones only the metrics identifier by one second; the other deadlines keep precedence. Errors stay explicit and do not undo the cleanup already performed.

The internal wake point registers a single replaceable token and uses the same exclusive access as registration and sampling. The outcome distinguishes a completed reset from a postponed one. A second wake during the reset stays pending; a read started before the wake returns stale evaluations and does not change health or admissions. Charges already accounted for are not refunded. The reset breaks measurement continuity without a native read, keeps credit/debt, the admission block and history, and requires a new baseline. The connection to a qualified macOS source remains separate and blocked together with the launcher.

The verification of the tranche and its limits are kept in [CPU admission and deadlines](../superpowers/verification/2026-09-21-addon-cpu-admission.md).


## Retry after a classified exit

Only an unexpected physical exit classified by the host counts as a crash. The default caller stays unclassified: a closed connection, an absent PID or a stop request do not prove a crash. An exact session issued at the handoff also makes it possible to handle exits that precede the first metric. Closing the connection can keep the session solely to classify the physical exit; expected stops cancel it.

With current demand, the first three crashes produce single-use tickets at 1, 5 and 30 seconds; the fourth puts the version in quarantine. Demand is a canonical command that is still queued and not expired, or a valid broker interest for the verified provider. Retained publications, static dependencies and work already delivered do not stand in for it. The shared cleanup serializes the crash decision with broker changes; a crash not yet decided prevents new starts and replacement CPU sessions. After every wait, authority and the effective deadline are rechecked.

Retries share the actions/cold start identifier, with no further timers or queues. A pending ticket blocks ordinary starts. At the deadline, the same admission checks demand before the reservations and after the waits, prepares the consumption and the new session on a copy of the store, and makes them canonical immediately before the start is delivered. An earlier rejection keeps the attempt and returns the reservations; a rejected delivery spends the ticket without replay. Loss of demand removes the ticket. Attempts that are due but temporarily unavailable are rearmed with a bounded postponement.

Disable and stop cancel sessions and retries; sleep/wake cancels tickets and suspended crash decisions, preserving the sessions of processes that are still alive and the CPU debt. A consumer without a process can record a delegated moderate through its exact ticket: `keep` keeps the ticket, quarantine removes it. It does not create an alternative session that would erase the retry. The [verification of the tranche](../superpowers/verification/2026-09-22-addon-transitive-cpu.md) distinguishes these internal contracts from the qualification of the launcher and of OS signals.

## Recipients of delegated CPU

The internal coordinator accepts a bounded list of `ProcessMetricDelegatedAttribution`, associated with the complete physical binding. The host must prove that the listed identities have canonical interest in the interval: the coordinator validates their form, capacity and binding, but does not derive that authority from PIDs or addon payloads. Validation and admission of new accounts are atomic and precede every read; no recipient is omitted under pressure and no account is evicted.

Each interval is charged to the direct owner and to every distinct consumer, using the same persistent account and the pre-existing arithmetic operations. Multiple interests, repeated paths or the owner itself in the list do not multiply the same cost. Physical rows are read and reported only once. Multiple rows contribute to a single classification per identity/round; a missing row prevents completeness, while another row can already prove new usage beyond credit. Arithmetic errors stay persistent and do not reopen the account.

The runtime now connects the broker and the coordinator through a single registry limited to the catalog's verified identities. For each binding the registry accumulates, between two observations, the union of the recipients reachable along chains that are simultaneously active. It does not compute the closure of the historical union of edges: two links that never overlapped do not create a chain. It deduplicates identities, diamonds and cycles. The canonical interest boundary and every physical read are synchronized; no lock spans an asynchronous operation. Disconnect and exit can keep interests; revocation, expiry and removal withdraw them.

The coordinator atomically pre-admits the domain's accounts, rejects additional manual attributions in this mode, and reports the exact contributors of each recipient. After the read the runtime rechecks catalog, incarnation, binding, session and wake, both for the recipient and for every contributor. A consumer without a process can receive delegated CPU; a present process without a valid measurement of its own does not reopen admissions on the strength of the credit measured on the providers alone. New acquisitions also check the consumer's pause; reuse of interests and work already admitted remain available. After a v1.4 commit followed by a rejection, the admission ack and the subsequent indeterminate outcome are kept.

The [choice on chains](../../.scratch/cascade-product/issues/57-addon-transitive-cpu-attribution.md) and the [verified runtime wiring](../../.scratch/cascade-product/issues/61-addon-runtime-transitive-cpu.md) keep the contract and the evidence: 1,195 tests/112 suites PASS before the subsequent retry integration. They are not evidence of per-request causality, OS identity or native stop. The earlier [delegated accounting](../superpowers/verification/2026-09-22-addon-delegated-cpu.md) documents the manual path still available to callers without the registry.

## Provider RAM profile

The [approved policy](../../.scratch/cascade-product/issues/65-addon-provider-memory-policy.md) uses MiB of 1,048,576 bytes and the footprint of the provider process alone. A current measurement above 64 MiB and up to 96 MiB inclusive opens a moderate episode: it closes new admissions and counts one incident on entry. Further high measurements, a missing datum or the wake keep the episode open. A valid measurement of its own at 64 MiB or less closes it. A new physical incarnation gets a new episode, but does not remove the pause retained per owner until it demonstrates recovery.

CPU and RAM have independent pauses and share the per-version health history. In the same round, a new CPU moderate and a RAM moderate count as a single incident; three moderates within the closed 300-second window keep the existing quarantine behavior. RAM recovery does not remove the CPU pause or the quarantine. The checks on new jobs and on canonical interests respect both pauses; work already admitted keeps its deadlines and existing interests can be reused.

Above 96 MiB, a request for an expected stop of the exact incarnation takes precedence, without adding a moderate in the same round or generating a crash retry. The request does not prove that the process has already exited: the physical reservations remain until the exit is observed. No cache-release message is introduced.

RAM uses only its own validated physical row, even when a computable CPU interval is missing. A stale delegated CPU contributor does not invalidate an own RAM measurement that is still current; authority and provenance are evaluated before the round's effects. The order of the providers must not cause a CPU charge to be lost because another owner has already requested a RAM stop.

These rules concern the internal provider runtime. Remote UI, audio, identity and native process control keep their respective prerequisites.
